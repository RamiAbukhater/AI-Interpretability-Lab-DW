Homework 2: Mapping persona space (Section 1)

Due: DUE_DATE_HW2

What to do
Read concepts 1 to 5. Complete every Section 1 exercise in the notebook. Then set SMOKE_TEST = False and run Section 1 with Gemma 2 9B.
Notebook part
Section 1, Mapping Persona Space: generating responses, (optionally) judging, extracting activations and persona vectors, cosine similarity, PCA, and the 27B reference plots.
Expected output
A centered cosine-similarity heatmap with visible block structure; a PCA scatter plot whose PC1 separates default and Assistant-like personas from fantastical ones, coloured by projection onto the axis; the axis norm printed as 1.0000.
Submit
  1. The centered cosine-similarity heatmap (9B run).
  2. The PCA plot, with the explained-variance percentages visible on the axes.
  3. A short paragraph (150 to 250 words) interpreting them. Which personas cluster together? What share of variance does PC1 explain? Which personas sit at each end, and does anything surprise you? How does the ordering compare with the paper's 27B reference plots (patterns only, not numbers)?

Homework 3: Drift and steering (Section 2)

Due: DUE_DATE_HW3

What to do
Read concepts 6 and 7. Complete the Section 2 exercises up to and including the steering experiments, using the 9B model.
Notebook part
Section 2, Steering along the Assistant Axis: ConversationAnalyzer, rate_delusion_risk, the drift plots, generate_with_steering, and the steering experiments. Activation capping is not part of this assignment.
Expected output
Drift plots for the two delusion transcripts, with a negative printed correlation between projection and risk for at least one of them; steered generations that change noticeably between negative, zero, and positive \(\alpha\).
Submit
  1. Your steering outputs: for at least three personas and at least four values of \(\alpha\) (including 0), the generated text in a table or as notebook output. Include the role-adoption prompt results too.
  2. A short reflection (200 to 300 words). At what \(\alpha\) did behaviour clearly change, and at what \(\alpha\) did text fall apart? Did steering away produce nonhuman or mystical personas, as the paper reports for Gemma? Do your drift plots and steering results together convince you the axis is causal? What would you need to check to be more sure?

Then: propose an extension

Due: DUE_DATE_EXTENSION

In one or two paragraphs, propose one experiment that goes beyond the notebook. Say what you would change, what you expect to see and why, and what result would surprise you. You do not need to run it yet. Some directions to get you thinking:

  • Other models. Does a newer model (for example Gemma 3 or Gemma 4, if it fits on Kaggle) have an Assistant Axis? Is it as dominant?
  • Other layers. Compute the axis at several layers. Where is it most aligned with PC1, and where does steering work best?
  • New personas. Add personas the notebook does not include. Where do "customer service agent", "therapist", or "an AI with no restrictions" land?
  • New steering targets. Steer with the difference between two specific personas (for example ghost minus consultant) instead of the Assistant Axis. Is the effect different?
  • A control. Steer with a random direction of the same norm and compare with the axis.
  • The judge filter. Turn on RUN_JUDGE_FILTER. How much do the persona vectors and PC1 change when only fully in-character responses are kept?
