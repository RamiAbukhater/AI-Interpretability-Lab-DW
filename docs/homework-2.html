Homework 2

Propose an extension

You have reproduced the core of the paper on Gemma 2 9B. Now propose one experiment that goes beyond the notebook. The best proposals become the group's next projects.

Due: DUE_DATE_HW2

How to submit

SUBMISSION_METHOD One document, named lastname_hw2_proposal. You do not need to run the experiment yet. Complete Homework 1 first; the proposal should build on what you saw there.

What to write

One to two paragraphs, roughly 250 to 400 words, that answer:

  1. The question. What do you want to find out, in one sentence?
  2. The change. What exactly would you change in the notebook: a model, a layer, a set of personas, a steering vector, a dataset, a metric? Name the cells or functions involved.
  3. The prediction. What do you expect to see, and why? Point to something in the paper or in your Homework 1 results.
  4. The surprise. What result would surprise you, and what would it mean?
  5. Feasibility. Will it fit on Kaggle's free T4 x2 and within your weekly GPU quota? If not, how would you shrink it?

Directions to get you thinking

Pick one of these or come up with your own.

  • Other models. Does a newer model (for example Gemma 3 or Gemma 4, if it fits on Kaggle) have an Assistant Axis? Is it as dominant?
  • Other layers. Compute the axis at several layers. Where is it most aligned with PC1, and where does steering work best?
  • New personas. Add personas the notebook does not include. Where do "customer service agent", "therapist", or "an AI with no restrictions" land?
  • New steering targets. Steer with the difference between two specific personas (for example ghost minus consultant) instead of the Assistant Axis. Is the effect different?
  • A control. Steer with a random direction of the same norm and compare it with the axis.
  • The judge filter. Turn on RUN_JUDGE_FILTER. How much do the persona vectors and PC1 change when only fully in-character responses are kept?
  • New conversations. Write your own multi-turn conversation in a domain the paper did not test and see whether it drifts.

What makes a strong proposal

  • It changes one thing at a time, so the result is interpretable.
  • It makes a prediction that could turn out wrong.
  • It includes a comparison: a baseline, a control, or the original result.
  • It is small enough to run in a week on free hardware.
